# <span class="masthead-store">H&amp;M</span> Return Policy: How Long Do You Have, and What Does It Cost?
{: .masthead}

The short answer is 30 days. H&M counts those days from the day your parcel is delivered, not the day it leaves the warehouse. The part that costs you money is elsewhere: how you send it back changes how much of your refund you actually keep.

## The two clocks

| Where you bought it | How long you have | Counting from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online | 30 days | The delivery date |
| In an H&M store | 30 days | The date on your receipt |

This is worth knowing if you also shop at [Zara]({{ '/return-policies/zara/' | relative_url }}), which counts the same 30 days from the shipping date instead. Same headline number, and often a week of real difference.

## Postmarked, not received

For a mail-in return the deadline is the postmark, not the day the parcel reaches the warehouse. Handing it over on day 30 counts. That only helps if you actually get to a post office in time, so it is a small mercy rather than a real extension.

## Taking it into a store is free, posting it is not

Returning an online order to an H&M store costs nothing. Mailing it back has a flat fee taken straight out of your refund rather than charged to you separately, which is why the refund can come back smaller than you expected.

H&M's own returns page put that fee at $5.99 in the United States when this page was last checked. Fees change, so treat the figure shown to you in the returns portal as the real one.

## A store purchase has to go back to a store

Only online orders can be posted back. Anything bought in a shop has to be returned to a shop, which matters if you bought something while travelling.

## The receipt matters more here than in some shops

H&M asks for valid proof of purchase with the tags still attached. If you are an H&M member and were signed in when you bought the item, the purchase sits in your account and works as the receipt.

With no proof at all it becomes a decision for the store. Where a return is allowed, it is usually store credit at the item's most recent selling price rather than what you originally paid, which can be a lot less if the item has since gone on sale.

## What cannot go back

- Swimwear and underwear once the hygiene seal is broken.
- Pierced jewellery.
- Gift cards and anything that has been altered.
- Some designer collaboration pieces carry a much shorter window than 30 days, so check the tag rather than assuming.

## Holiday returns are not guaranteed

H&M has run extended windows in past years for purchases made in November and December, letting them go back into late January. It is announced annually rather than being part of the standard policy, so confirm it at the time of purchase instead of assuming it applies.

## When the money actually arrives

Refunds go back to the original payment method. The shipping and handling you paid on the way out is not returned, so a small order sent back by post can cost you more than it gives back once the return fee is taken off as well.

The wait is really three separate stages, and only the middle one belongs to H&M. The parcel has to reach them, they have to receive and check it, and then your bank has to post the credit. People count from the day they dropped the parcel off, which is why the money feels late even when nothing has gone wrong.

Taking the item into a store removes the first stage entirely, which is the single biggest thing you can do to speed it up.
