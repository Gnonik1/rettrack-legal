# <span class="masthead-store">Shein</span> Return Policy: 30 Days, and Only One Free Return
{: .masthead}

The deadline is not what catches people out at Shein. The fee is. You get one free return for each order, and every parcel you send back after that has a charge taken out of your refund, even when the items came from that same order.

## One free return per order, then you pay

Shein gives you a prepaid label for the first return from any order, with nothing deducted. The second parcel from that same order is charged, and so is the third. The fee is per parcel, not per item, so sending five things back in one box costs the same as sending one.

That is a different shape of cost from [Zara]({{ '/return-policies/zara/' | relative_url }}) and [H&M]({{ '/return-policies/hm/' | relative_url }}), where every posted return is charged, and from [Zalando]({{ '/return-policies/zalando/' | relative_url }}), where none of them are.

## The trap is the second parcel

The way people lose money here is simple and very common. You order eight things, try five on over a weekend, and send those back with the free label. On Wednesday you decide about the other three and post them too. That second parcel is charged, even though every item came from the same order.

The fix costs nothing: try everything on before you print the first label, and send it all back together.

## When the 30 days start

Here the guides disagree with each other. Some say the clock runs from the day you ordered, others from the day the parcel was delivered. Older articles still quote 35 or 45 days, which Shein has since shortened.

We are not going to add another confident number to that pile. What is not in dispute is that Shein shows the exact deadline beside each item under My Orders in the app. That date reflects your country and your order, and it is the only version worth trusting.

The difference matters more here than at most shops, because Shein parcels often spend one to three weeks in transit. If the clock started at the order date, a slow delivery could eat half the window before you have opened the bag.

## What cannot go back

The non-returnable list is longer than at most shops, and it is mostly about hygiene.

- Underwear, lingerie, sleepwear and bodysuits.
- Jewellery.
- Beauty products once opened.
- Anything customised or given as a free gift.
- Items marked as final sale or clearance.

The product page says whether an item is returnable, and it says it before you buy rather than after. On a large haul that is worth a few seconds per item.

## Condition

Items need to be unworn, unwashed and in the condition they arrived in, with the original tags still attached. Shoes, accessories and electronics have stricter rules about keeping the original packaging.

## Do not mix orders in one box

Each return label is tied to a specific order. Putting items from two different orders into the same parcel means the warehouse scans a label that does not expect them, and the unlinked items may simply not be refunded.

## When the money actually arrives

Shein gives you a choice when you start the return: back to the payment method you used, or into your Shein wallet. The wallet is the faster of the two because it skips the bank entirely. The card is slower, and it is the right choice if you do not plan to shop there again.

Original shipping and handling is not refunded, and where a return fee applies it comes out of the amount rather than being billed to you separately.

Beyond that it is the usual three stages: the parcel travelling back, Shein receiving and inspecting it, and then either the wallet crediting almost immediately or your bank taking its own time.
