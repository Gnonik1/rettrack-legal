# <span class="masthead-store">Trendyol</span> Return Policy: The Window Depends on Where You Are
{: .masthead}

Every other shop on this site has one number. Trendyol has several, because the window is set per region rather than globally. Before you count anything, work out which Trendyol site your order came from.

## The window depends on the region

| Where you ordered from | How long you have |
| --- | --- |
| Europe | 30 days from delivery |
| Gulf region | 15 days from delivery |
| Anywhere else | Check your own country's site |

Returns are free in both. The 30 day European window is on a par with most of the high street, while 15 days is genuinely short, and short enough that a parcel left unopened for a week has used half of it.

## The second deadline nobody mentions

This is the part that catches people, and it is in Trendyol's own policy rather than hidden anywhere. Requesting the return is not the same as sending it. Once your request is approved you are issued a return label, and that label is valid for 14 days on its own count.

So a Gulf customer who requests a return on day 14 of 15 does not then have one day left. They have a fresh 14 days to actually get the parcel to a drop-off point. It works the other way too: request early and then leave the parcel in the hall, and the label can expire even though the original window has not.

## It is a marketplace, so the seller matters

Trendyol lists its own stock alongside thousands of third-party sellers. The platform sets the baseline rules, but the seller handles the item, and a product page can mark something as non-returnable that would ordinarily be fine. The product page is the version that applies, not the general policy.

If something arrives faulty, missing parts or simply wrong, that route stays open even on an item marked non-returnable. It goes through Trendyol customer support rather than the standard returns flow, and there is a longer window for it.

## Return it from the country it was delivered to

The parcel has to be sent back from the same country as the delivery address, because the return crosses a customs border and the paperwork is built around that address. Carrying something home from a trip and posting it from there is the way to have a return refused before anyone has looked at the item.

## Only Trendyol's own label works

Returns are only accepted with a return label generated in Trendyol's system, which already carries the address details. Sending a parcel back with your own courier and your own label means it arrives with nothing linking it to your order.

## What cannot go back

The hygiene exclusions are the usual ones: underwear, swimwear and bikinis are only accepted sealed and unopened. Food and drink, jewellery and personalised items are outside the policy as well.

## Coins or the card

When you start the return you choose where the money goes: back to the payment method you used, or into Trendyol Coins. Coins land faster because nothing has to pass through a bank. The card is slower and it is the right choice if you do not plan to order again.

## When the money actually arrives

Trendyol's own policy says it can take up to 14 business days from the courier collecting the parcel for the return to be received and processed. That is before your bank does anything, so treat it as the start of the wait rather than the whole of it.

The refund is only issued once the item has been checked, and on a marketplace that check belongs to whoever sold it. A return handled by a smaller seller can sit longer than one fulfilled by Trendyol itself.

If you chose Coins, the last stage disappears and the balance appears once the return is approved. If you chose the card, your bank adds its own time on top, and that part is outside everyone's control.
