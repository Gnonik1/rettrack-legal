# <span class="masthead-store">Temu</span> Return Policy: 90 Days, Counted From the Wrong End
{: .masthead}

Ninety days is the longest window on this site by a distance. Two things quietly shorten it. The count starts the day you place the order, not the day the parcel lands, and once you request a return you have a much shorter deadline to actually post it.

## The count starts at checkout

Temu's own policy counts 90 days from the date of purchase. On a shop where delivery can take a couple of weeks, that is a fortnight of the window gone before you have opened anything.

Even after that, 90 days is far longer than anywhere else here, including [Nike]({{ '/return-policies/nike/' | relative_url }}) at 60. It is the opposite end of the same trick [Zara]({{ '/return-policies/zara/' | relative_url }}) uses, where a generous-sounding number starts counting earlier than you expect.

## You have 14 days to actually post it

Requesting a return is not the same as sending one. Once the request goes in you are issued a label, and the parcel has to be postmarked within 14 days. Miss that and the return can be voided even though you are nowhere near day 90.

The practical version: do not open a return until you are ready to take the parcel out.

## One free return per order

Temu covers return shipping on the first return from any order, however many items are in that one parcel. A second return from the same order has a fee taken out of the refund.

This is the same structure [Shein]({{ '/return-policies/shein/' | relative_url }}) uses, and it costs people money the same way: decide on everything before you print the first label, then send it all back together.

Where the second parcel is going back because the item arrived damaged, defective or wrong, it is worth contacting support rather than paying the fee. That is not the situation the charge is meant for.

## Not everything gets 90 days

Electronics carry a shorter window than the headline number, and it varies by seller rather than being fixed. Anything marked final sale or non-returnable on the product page is out from the start.

The product page is the version that applies. On a shop where an order can run to twenty items, that is worth a few seconds each rather than an assumption across the lot.

## What cannot go back

Clothing that has been worn, washed, or had its tags or hygiene sticker removed is refused, and so is anything returned as an incomplete set. Underwear, opened earbuds, customised products and some free gift items are outside the policy entirely.

Keep the packaging until you have decided. Original condition here includes the barcode, and a return without it is harder to match to your order.

## Credits or the card

You choose where the refund goes when you start the return: back to the payment method you used, or into Temu credits. Credits are much faster because nothing passes through a bank, and they do not expire.

The trade is that a refund to credits generally cannot be reversed once processed. If you are not planning to order again, take the slower route.

## When the money actually arrives

The refund is issued after the warehouse has received the parcel and the items have passed inspection, so the wait starts when it arrives rather than when you post it.

Temu's own policy puts the bank stage at 5 to 14 business days, and says it can run to 30 depending on the institution. That part is outside everyone's control.

The original shipping fee, any import tax and any insurance you paid are not refunded unless the return is Temu's fault or the seller's. On a small order that can be a real share of what you expected back.
